# Section I Solution: Software License Review

The assessments below use the assumptions stated in the exercise. Equivalent explanations and other concrete corrections are acceptable if supported by the relevant license conditions.

| Scenario | License and type | Acceptable or mistake? | Reason and supporting license provision | Required correction, or why none is needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A: A Useful Repository** | No license / no reuse permission stated; no open source license type can be assigned. | **Mistake** | Public availability and free download do not establish permission to copy substantial code into a product and redistribute it. Recording a URL does not supply the missing permission. See [Repositories without a license](https://choosealicense.com/no-permission/). | Obtain an explicit license from the rights holder that permits the intended use and meet its conditions, or remove the parser and use an appropriately licensed alternative or an independent implementation. |
| **B: The Commercial Desktop Application** | MIT: permissive; no copyleft requirement. | **Acceptable** | The [MIT permission grant](https://opensource.org/license/mit) permits commercial use and distribution without requiring publication of application source. Its notice condition requires the copyright and permission notices to accompany copies or substantial portions. The scenario supplies them. | No correction is required under the stated conditions. Continue including the library's complete license and copyright notice in every customer package. |
| **C: Cleaning Up the Headers** | MIT: permissive; no copyleft requirement. | **Mistake** | The [MIT notice condition](https://opensource.org/license/mit) still applies to the substantial copied code after renaming and minor modifications. No required notices accompany the distributed copy. | Restore the original copyright notice and complete MIT text and ensure they accompany the copied code in distributions. Retaining them in the source and supplying a third-party licenses file avoids losing this information. |
| **D: The Downloadable Utility** | GPL-3.0-only: strong copyleft; distributed covered modifications remain subject to GPL source and licensing obligations. | **Mistake** | [GPLv3 Section 6](https://opensource.org/license/gpl-3.0) requires compliant access to Corresponding Source when object code is conveyed. Supplying only the license is insufficient. Charging nothing does not remove this requirement. | For example, supply the complete Corresponding Source alongside the executable with equivalent download access under Section 6(d), and meet the applicable modified-work conditions of Section 5. Include needed build materials, not just the changed files. If GreenByte will not comply, stop distributing that modified utility and replace it or obtain separate permission. |
| **E: An Internal Analysis Tool** | GPL-3.0-only: strong copyleft; internal use does not change the license type. | **Acceptable; the reviewer's claim is mistaken** | [GPLv3 Section 2](https://opensource.org/license/gpl-3.0) permits making, running, and propagating covered works without conveying them, subject to its conditions. The stated use stays within one organization and does not require public release of modifications. | No public source release is required on these facts. Reassess the obligations if copies are later conveyed outside the organization. |
| **F: The Reporting Component** | Apache-2.0: permissive; notice and modification requirements do not create copyleft. | **Acceptable** | [Apache 2.0 Section 4(a)–(d)](https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0) covers the license copy, modification statements, retained source notices, and relevant existing `NOTICE` attributions. These requirements are addressed. Section 4 also permits additional or different terms for modifications or the derivative work as a whole, subject to continued compliance. | No correction is required under the stated conditions. Apache 2.0 does not require publishing the proprietary application's source. Preserve the described release materials and modification statements. |
| **G: The Updated Exporter** | Apache-2.0: permissive; notice and modification requirements do not create copyleft. | **Mistake** | [Apache 2.0 Section 4(b)](https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0) requires modified files to carry prominent notices that they were changed. An inaccessible Git history does not satisfy this file-level requirement. | Add a prominent modification statement to each changed file before distribution, identifying that GreenByte changed it. Keep the other required license and attribution materials. |
| **H: A Small Open Source Project** | MIT: permissive; commercial proprietary reuse is permitted subject to its conditions. | **Acceptable; the colleague's expectation is mistaken** | The [MIT permission grant](https://opensource.org/license/mit) already authorizes commercial reuse and sale subject to its notice condition. It does not require a separate fee, permission request, or publication of the recipient's product source. The other company retains the required notices. | No correction is required from the other company on these facts. Explain to the colleague that the authorized MIT release grants these reuse rights. |

None of these scenarios uses a weak copyleft license. For comparison, MPL-2.0 is weak copyleft at the file level: its source-sharing requirements apply to covered files rather than automatically to all files in a larger application. See the [license comparison](https://choosealicense.com/licenses/).
